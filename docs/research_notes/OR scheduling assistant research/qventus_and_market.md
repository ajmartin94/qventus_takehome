# Qventus Perioperative / OR Scheduling Products, AI Assistant Strategy, and the OR Scheduling Competitive Landscape

Research date: 2026-10-14. Nearly every outcome metric below is **vendor-reported** (press releases, case studies, vendor blogs) unless explicitly marked otherwise. Independent evidence is scarce. The exceptions are KLAS (small samples) and two peer-institution studies on Apella and Opmed.

---

## 1. What does Qventus sell for perioperative / OR, which workflows does it automate, and for which users?

### Takeaway
Qventus sells two perioperative products. The first is the **Surgical Growth Solution**: OR access and growth tooling that grew out of the 2021 "Perioperative Solution." It covers block-release nudges, open-time marketing to best-fit surgeons, a self-service open-time finder for surgeons and offices, robotics optimization, independent-surgeon clinic booking, and fax intake. The second is **Perioperative Care Coordination**, launched January 2025: AI teammates for pre-admission testing (PAT) readiness and cancellation reduction. The core users are OR schedulers, surgeons, surgeon-office/clinic schedulers, perioperative leaders, and PAT nurses.

### Cited Findings

**Origin: the "Perioperative Solution" (Oct 4, 2021)**
- Launched Oct 4, 2021 with three modules: **Access**, **Growth** and **Insights**. Qventus says it adds "2+ cases per OR per month" and "increases revenue by millions"; these are headline claims with no attribution. — [Qventus press release, Oct 2021](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-introduces-new-perioperative-solution-to-increase-prime-time-utilization-and-grow-case-volume/)
- **Access / Block Release:** ML predicts *up to a month ahead* which blocks are unlikely to be used. Behavioral science then presents data and incentives that nudge surgeons and schedulers to release all or part of the block. — [same](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-introduces-new-perioperative-solution-to-increase-prime-time-utilization-and-grow-case-volume/)
- **Access / Case rescheduling:** reviews scheduled cases and suggests earlier dates, freeing time weeks in advance. **Slot and room optimization** uses ML to shift slots and rooms for robotic vs. non-robotic cases. — [same](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-introduces-new-perioperative-solution-to-increase-prime-time-utilization-and-grow-case-volume/)
- **Growth / Available Time Outreach:** ML finds the best-fit surgeon for open time from practice patterns and past performance, then automatically offers the time. It weighs strategic priorities such as targeted service lines and moving low-acuity volume. — [same](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-introduces-new-perioperative-solution-to-increase-prime-time-utilization-and-grow-case-volume/)
- **Growth / TimeFinder:** employed and community surgeons and their schedulers see open OR time in a "reservation-style" interface. They can also see available time in colleagues' blocks and submit case requests. **No EHR login is needed, and it replaces the separate faxed request form.** — [same](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-introduces-new-perioperative-solution-to-increase-prime-time-utilization-and-grow-case-volume/)
- **Insights:** *Opportunity Finder* gives real-time tracking of case growth and scheduling accuracy. *Surgical Practice Improvement* shares efficiency insights with surgeons. — [same](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-introduces-new-perioperative-solution-to-increase-prime-time-utilization-and-grow-case-volume/)
- 2021 deployments named: UAMS, Saint Luke's, Bon Secours Mercy Health, CommonSpirit Health, M Health Fairview and Mercy. UAMS's Chief Perioperative Officer said it "rapidly added hundreds of new cases." — [same](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-introduces-new-perioperative-solution-to-increase-prime-time-utilization-and-grow-case-volume/)
- Saint Luke's case study (PDF, 2024) describes the Access Module and Available Time Outreach: when an OR slot becomes available, an algorithm automatically offers the time. — [Qventus Saint Luke's case study PDF](https://www.qventus.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Qventus_Case-Study_Perioperative_St-Lukes.pdf) (seen via search snippet only)

**Current naming: "Surgical Growth Solution" assistants (2025–2026)**
- The Erlanger blog (Feb 2026) names the current assistants:
  - **TimeFinder:** surgeons and schedulers see relevant OR time "in seconds" without logging into the EHR. Case length is predicted from each surgeon's history.
  - **Capacity Assistant:** prompts block release weeks in advance; ML flags likely-unused blocks up to a month ahead.
  - **Strategic Marketing Assistant:** markets time to best-fit surgeons, "replacing manual calls," and prioritizes service-line and robotic growth.
  - **Robotics Optimizer:** ML-driven outreach marketing time to robotic surgeons.
  - Nudges are sent only when they fit a surgeon's preferences and case mix.
  — [Qventus blog: How Erlanger adds 220 surgical cases per month](https://www.qventus.com/resources/blog/how-erlanger-adds-220-surgical-cases-per-month-with-ai-powered-surgical-operations/)
- AI teammates added in May 2025:
  - **Strategic Control Panel:** points AI teammates at goals such as robotics, OR efficiency, site-of-care and service-line growth.
  - **Clinic Scheduler Assistant:** integrates into *independent surgeons' clinic EMRs* so clinic schedulers can book cases at the health system. Median turnaround is **22 minutes**.
  - **Document Assistant:** digitizes, categorizes and summarizes perioperative scheduling faxes and routes them downstream. Qventus says it saves **~60 OR-scheduler hours per month**.
  - **Robotics Assistant:** for da Vinci programs. Claims >10% incremental robotic volume and an 11% drop in non-robotic cases in robotic rooms.
  - **Site of Care Optimization:** moves low-acuity cases to ASCs.
  - **Enhanced write-back:** bi-directional EHR integration that removes double data entry. Claims **~100 hours saved per OR scheduler per month**.
  — [Qventus press release, May 13, 2025](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-ai-teammates-transform-the-perioperative-care-journey-driving-strategic-surgical-growth-and-winning-several-new-clients-and-enterprise-wide-expansions/)
- **Perioperative Care Coordination (PCC)**, launched January 2025:
  - Gives every PAT team member AI teammates ("high-tech admins") plus a **Patient Concierge**, with the goal of cutting 72-hour cancellations.
  - Innovation Cohort: HonorHealth, Allina, Northwestern Medicine, Ardent Health and UAMS.
  - Qventus claims "up to 40% reduction" in cancellations and a "50% productivity boost" for care operations teams.
  — [Qventus PCC launch release](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-launches-perioperative-care-coordination-solution-to-alleviate-the-administrative-burden-on-clinicians-reduce-unnecessary-surgery-cancellations-and-optimize-patients-pre-and-post-surgery/); [May 2025 release](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-ai-teammates-transform-the-perioperative-care-journey-driving-strategic-surgical-growth-and-winning-several-new-clients-and-enterprise-wide-expansions/)
- PCC assistants can **make and receive voice calls**, "listen for and act on follow-ups within the EHR," talk to patients by SMS, chatbot or email, read and interpret chart notes, and track reminders. — [Qventus: Allina inaugural AI Solution Factory client, Dec 9, 2025](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-announces-allina-health-as-its-inaugural-ai-solution-factory-client/)
- Users named across releases: health-system leaders, OR schedulers, surgeons, PAT staff, and independent surgeons and their clinic schedulers. — [May 2025 release](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-ai-teammates-transform-the-perioperative-care-journey-driving-strategic-surgical-growth-and-winning-several-new-clients-and-enterprise-wide-expansions/); [Erlanger blog](https://www.qventus.com/resources/blog/how-erlanger-adds-220-surgical-cases-per-month-with-ai-powered-surgical-operations/)
- Qventus's other product lines are **Inpatient Capacity** and, as of Feb 26, 2026, a **Care Gap and Coding Automation Suite**. — [Business Wire, Feb 26, 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260226793642/en/Qventus-Launches-Care-Gap-and-Coding-Automation-Suite-to-Improve-Patient-Care-and-Reimbursement-Revenue) (headline only, not fetched)

### Inferences
- Qventus's OR product is mainly **supply-side liquidity**: it frees block time early and markets it to the right surgeon. It also reduces the friction for surgeon offices to request and book open time (TimeFinder, Clinic Scheduler Assistant, Document Assistant for fax). A chat assistant that checks OR availability and books a case for a scheduling coordinator fits directly on top of TimeFinder plus write-back plus the Clinic Scheduler Assistant. Framing the take-home as "conversational TimeFinder + booking with EHR write-back" matches the actual product line.
- Qventus's own language suggests the take-home should include these elements:
  - case length predicted from the surgeon's history
  - best-fit open time that respects block ownership and release rules
  - robotics room constraints
  - a "no EHR login needed" experience
  - removing faxed request forms
- Plausible personas: a hospital OR scheduler, a clinic scheduler at an independent practice, or a surgeon.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the current Surgical Growth product page; it returned only a Lottie animation payload. Feature names therefore come from press releases and blogs, not a current product spec.
- No public detail on the exact booking mechanics, such as whether the Clinic Scheduler Assistant creates a case request or a confirmed booking in the hospital EHR, or how approval works.

## 3. How does Qventus describe EHR integration (Epic, Oracle Health) and its "Solution Factory" / AI platform approach?

### Takeaway
Qventus describes itself as an EHR-agnostic **"system of action" on top of systems of record**, with "deep," bi-directional EHR integration and write-back. It almost never names Epic in its OR materials. The **AI Solution Factory** (Sept 2025) is a co-development program built on a shared "Qventus AI Platform." That platform has reusable assistant skills: chart mining, risk determination, care-gap orchestration, intelligent document (fax) management, Patient Concierge (voice, text, email) and Call Center Assistant. An embedded Qventus AI transformation team supports each client.

### Cited Findings
- The AI Solution Factory debuted Sept 18, 2025 at Qventus's QLive conference (Dallas). It lets strategic partners co-develop custom AI Operational Assistants on the **Qventus AI Platform**. The platform "integrates deeply with hospital EHRs" and uses "localized and probabilistic machine learning, behavioral science, and conversational AI to automate end-to-end workflows." — [Qventus, Sept 18, 2025](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-drives-next-wave-of-healthcare-ai-innovation-with-debut-of-ai-solution-factory-and-releases-new-roi-outcomes-from-health-systems/)
- Platform "assistant skills":
  - **Chart Mining**, which cites its sources.
  - **Continuous Risk Determination.**
  - **Care Gap Orchestration.**
  - **Intelligent Document Management:** faxes, emails and inbound/outbound communications routed to the right team.
  - **Patient Concierge:** voice, text or email; collects intake and schedules appointments.
  - **Call Center Assistant:** prepares clinicians for calls, gives real-time suggestions and automates follow-ups.
  The assistants "speak, hear, read, gather, understand, and write." Named use cases include surgical prior authorization and referral automation. — [same](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-drives-next-wave-of-healthcare-ai-innovation-with-debut-of-ai-solution-factory-and-releases-new-roi-outcomes-from-health-systems/)
- AI teammates "combine structured and unstructured data and take autonomous action within existing EHR workflows." An on-site Qventus AI transformation team identifies use cases with frontline and IT staff. Qventus calls itself "a system of action that sits on top of a hospital's enterprise systems of record." — [HonorHealth release, Sept 17, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/honorhealth-selects-qventus-ai-solution-130000232.html)
- Solution Factory clients: **Allina Health** (inaugural, Dec 9, 2025) and **HonorHealth** (Sept 17, 2026). Allina's CDIO: "Being able to build AI assistants in partnership with our care team members is critical." — [Qventus Allina release](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-announces-allina-health-as-its-inaugural-ai-solution-factory-client/); [HonorHealth release](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/honorhealth-selects-qventus-ai-solution-130000232.html)
- Surgical Growth has "enhanced write-back functionality" built on "bi-directional EHR integration" to eliminate double entry. - TimeFinder works **without an EHR login** for surgeons and schedulers. — [Oct 2021 release](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-introduces-new-perioperative-solution-to-increase-prime-time-utilization-and-grow-case-volume/); [Erlanger blog](https://www.qventus.com/resources/blog/how-erlanger-adds-220-surgical-cases-per-month-with-ai-powered-surgical-operations/)
- None of the Qventus releases reviewed (May 2025, Sept 2025, Dec 2025, Sept 2026) name Epic or Oracle Health. — [Qventus releases above]
- A third-party comparison site says Qventus deploys as a cloud platform integrated into existing EHR workflows. It says rollout needs workflow mapping, integration work and change management. — [rfp.wiki Qventus vs Epic](https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/healthcare-life-sciences/healthcare/patient-throughput-and-capacity-management-software/qventus/epic) (third-party, low authority)

### Inferences
- Qventus avoids naming EHR vendors and stresses being "EHR-agnostic." Most large US health systems, and many Qventus clients (Northwestern, Allina, HonorHealth, Banner), run Epic. So the take-home could reasonably model:
  - an Epic-like OR schedule as the system of record, accessed through read APIs and write-back
  - the assistant acting as the "system of action" layer
  - booking writes flowing back with a human confirmation step
- A general integration guide notes that Epic's FHIR R4 Appointment resource is typically read and search only. Writing appointments may need other paths such as HL7 SIU or proprietary booking APIs. I could not tie this to a specific authoritative source, so verify it before relying on it. Even so, it is a realistic design consideration for a booking assistant, and a forward-deployed architect would be expected to raise it.

### Gaps
- No confirmation of whether Qventus is listed on Epic Showroom or holds Toolbox designations, or which integration methods it uses (HL7 v2 SIU/ADT feeds, FHIR, Epic APIs, RPA).
- No Qventus statement on Oracle Health (Cerner) support found.

## 4. Competitors and adjacent players: what do they offer for OR scheduling, and what outcomes do they claim?

### Takeaway
Qventus's most direct OR competitor is **LeanTaaS iQueue for Operating Rooms**. LeanTaaS acquired Hospital IQ in 2023 and claims 550+ hospitals and 5.7k+ ORs. It offers block release and request workflows, open-time matching, analytics, and since June 2025 an **iQueue for Surgical Clinics** product that reaches into the surgeon office. **Epic** is the platform risk: Emmie, Art and Penny agents (Aug 2025), and an **Agent Factory** previewed at HIMSS26 (Mar 2026), though no OpTime-specific agent was found. Newer AI entrants work on prediction and real-time OR management rather than booking:
- **Apella**: ambient computer vision plus "Horizon" scheduling optimization (Aug 2025).
- **Opmed.ai**: case-duration prediction and schedule optimization; Mayo and AtlantiCare (2026).
- **Caresyntax**: OR data and video analytics; its blog covers block marketplaces.

Surgeon-practice tools (**Surgimate**) and patient-facing voice agents (**Hippocratic AI**, Epic Emmie) cover adjacent pieces.

### Cited Findings

**LeanTaaS iQueue for Operating Rooms (Qventus's closest competitor)**
- Features listed on the product page:
  - Real-Time View
  - Daily Roster with "Push to Epic" (to Snapboard)
  - block release and request workflows
  - open-time matching of available time to likely surgeons
  - analytics and benchmarking
  - "predictive intelligence and workflow assistants"
  Users are surgeons and their offices, perioperative and pre-op teams, staffing coordinators and perioperative leaders. — [LeanTaaS OR product page](https://leantaas.com/products/operating-rooms/)
- Integration: "lightweight data feeds" covering Epic, Cerner and others. **Most workflows are read-only**; Daily Roster can push to Epic Snapboard. LeanTaaS positions iQueue as an optimization layer alongside the EHR. — [same](https://leantaas.com/products/operating-rooms/)
- Scale and claims: 550+ hospitals, 100+ health systems, 5.7k+ ORs, "5–7x ROI." Aggregate results: +5% case volume, −40% cancellations, +25% staffed-room utilization. — [same](https://leantaas.com/products/operating-rooms/)
- Customer results (vendor):
  - **University of Kansas Health System:** +20% block utilization, +4.8% prime-time utilization and −7% available rooms within one year.
  - **CommonSpirit:** more than $40M in the first 18 months.
  - **OHSU:** "unlocked more OR time in first week than prior full year."
  - **Lexington Medical Center:** +6% block utilization.

## 5. What gaps or unmet needs do products and customers call out?

### Takeaway
The pain points vendors and customers cite most often are:
- surgeon offices and clinic schedulers **hunting for OR time by phone and fax**
- faxed case-request forms that need manual entry, with **double data entry** into the EHR
- **late or no block release**, with more than 30% of OR time unused
- inaccurate case-length estimates that cause under-scheduling and delays
- booked cases that are not *ready* (authorization, H&P, consents), causing late cancellations
- independent surgeons taking cases to competitors when access is hard

### Inferences
- A realistic take-home scenario based on this evidence: a coordinator, either at a surgeon's office or a hospital OR scheduler acting for one, asks in natural language for time. The assistant would:
  1. Find candidate slots: open time, released blocks, and colleagues' blocks that are eligible for release. It respects block ownership, release windows and robot room or equipment constraints.
  2. Estimate case length from the surgeon's history rather than the requested duration.
  3. Check booking-policy rules: schedule closure and add-on rules, required prior authorization, H&P and consents.
  4. Create a hold or case request and write it back to the EHR after explicit confirmation.
  5. Optionally ingest a faxed booking sheet.
  This maps one-to-one to the Qventus capabilities above (TimeFinder, Capacity Assistant, Clinic Scheduler Assistant, Document Assistant, write-back) and to pain points customers name themselves.
- Metrics a demo could report:
  - time from request to booked (Qventus quotes a 22-minute median)
  - phone calls and faxes avoided
  - released hours re-filled
  - percentage of bookings with complete readiness data

### Gaps
- No independent survey (for example, from AORN or Becker's) quantifying how often surgeon offices still book by phone or fax was found within budget. Evidence for phone and fax pain is mostly vendor and customer anecdote.
- No KLAS 2025–2026 perioperative report details were accessible; they are paywalled.
