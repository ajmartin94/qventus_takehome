# Most OR scheduling work happens after booking

An OR scheduler's real job is keeping a surgical case's dependencies intact while it passes through a relay of at least six roles; finding a room is only one part of that job. The take-home's "surgical scheduling coordinator" title fits two of those roles. The **surgeon-practice coordinator** is a generalist who books OR time while also chasing prior authorization, clearances, implants and patient instructions. The **hospital central scheduler** is a rules-and-capacity specialist who checks each request and places it against block ownership, room capabilities and history-based durations. After them come the OR desk, the anesthesiologist-in-charge (AIC), the anesthesia scheduler and the block manager. Booking is one commit point in a weeks-long life, and most of the churn and failure comes afterward. In hospital audit data, **51.5% of ORs had their last case added or changed within two working days of surgery**. Spine surgeons reported that **58.5% of prior-authorization denials arrived within three days of the operation**. The interventions with measured results, readiness calls and chart reviews at 48 to 72 hours, roughly halved preventable late cancellations in quality-improvement and vendor reports. Settings, room types and specialties differ mostly in parameters, and only four behaviors need their own logic: emergencies that preempt electives, cardiac and transplant cases that must reserve an ICU bed with the room, parallel-room throughput, and site-of-service gating. Conversational and agentic AI pays off at the workflow's edges. It can turn faxes and calls into structured requests, chase open readiness items with named owners and deadlines, push changes to every downstream party, and answer the constant "is it on, is it ready?" traffic. A deterministic solver should own feasibility, a stable grid and worklist should own overview and verification, and people should own bumps, favors between surgeons and anything clinical. The earlier recommendation, to build fully a central coordinator booking a robotic right total knee, still holds. That path should now start from a pre-filled request, return structured option cards, and end with a readiness dependency graph and drafted outreach instead of a bare case number, with the rest of the lifecycle stubbed behind named interfaces.

## Six roles relay one case, and two of them answer to "coordinator"

On the surgeon's side sits the **practice or clinic surgical coordinator**, whose job is unusually broad. Duke Health's 2025 "Surgery Scheduler" description has this person verify each surgical posting (patient, procedure, diagnosis, site), enter orders, secure OR time with hospitals and surgery centers, arrange clearances and pre- and post-op appointments, verify benefits, obtain prior authorization, tell patients what they will owe, give verbal and written pre-op instructions, coordinate anesthesia needs and submit daily billing sheets. The role asks only for a high school diploma and two years of experience ([Duke job description](https://forms.hr.duke.edu/descriptions/duhs/select.php?ID=4655)). Orthopedic practices add implant-vendor liaison, consent documentation and pre-procedure calls ([Orthopaedic Specialty Group](https://osgpc.com/surgical-coordinator-careers/); [Florida OrthoCare](https://florthocare.com/jobs/orhtopedic-surgical-coordinator/)). The role often grows out of the clinic floor. At one Portland orthopedic group, medical assistants booked surgery between other duties until volume made the process fragile ([Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/hospital-management-administration/from-backlog-to-booked-how-one-orthopedic-practice-accelerated-surgical-case-velocity/)).

On the hospital side, the **central OR scheduler** specializes in rules and capacity. Postings describe building the surgical schedule in the EHR while weighing surgeon availability, procedure complexity, OR capacity and turnaround, keeping waitlists, confirming CPT codes and estimated times with surgeons' offices, and sending confirmation back once a case is placed ([University of Michigan posting](https://careers.umich.edu/node/271053); [Epic OpTime listings](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Epic-Optime); [HSS listing](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Surgery-Scheduler-Coordinator/--in-New-York)). Health systems increasingly centralize this team across hospitals, often working remotely, while each hospital keeps a local staffer for urgent and same-day add-ons ([LeanTaaS](https://leantaas.com/blog/centralized-surgical-scheduling-an-innovative-model-to-streamline-health-systems-address-case-backlogs/), vendor).

Today's schedule belongs to a third group: the **OR desk, charge nurse and AIC**, often called the "board runners." Allegheny General's OR Scheduling Coordinator works beside the charge nurse at the control desk ([AHN posting](https://zapply.jobs/jobs/c12f5790-9336-4d9c-88d8-d8d805fc7601/)). The coordinator maintains the current day's add-ons and cancellations, joins the huddle that approves tomorrow's schedule, calls floors for patient transport and checks in vendors. After 5 pm, Beth Israel Lahey routes add-ons and next-day bookings to an operations assistant who enters them and alerts PACU ([BILH posting](https://jobs.bilh.org/jobs/perioperative-ops-assistant-boston-ma-jr98421/)). Who actually runs the board varies. Nurses on one forum describe a charge nurse, charge anesthesiologist and charge CRNA moving resources together. Others describe a hospital where nothing moves unless the AIC and the nurse-in-charge agree ([AllNurses](https://allnurses.com/who-runs-your-operating-room-t560689/), anecdotal).

Three more roles complete the relay. **Anesthesia scheduling coordinators** map providers onto rooms: Brigham and Women's builds daily assignments for **more than 250 clinicians across 43 main ORs** plus off-site locations and triages every same-day change ([BWH posting](https://app.joinhandshake.com/jobs/4872728/share_preview)), and Johns Hopkins schedules **85 CRNAs across 75 anesthetizing locations a day** in QGenda ([JHH posting](https://spinalcord.dejobs.org/baltimore-md/clinical-scheduling-specialist/F3C6DB597005440E8063E87FA75B45D7/job)). **ASC schedulers** do the hospital job at smaller scale and with more personal negotiation; one Illinois orthopedic center refuses phone requests, emails surgeons about voluntary block releases, and phones them as the 72-hour release deadline approaches ([Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/asc-news/8-points-about-scheduling-cases-in-an-asc/)). **Perioperative business managers** look back at block utilization, push offices to use or release their time, and staff the block committee ([Texas Health posting](https://jobs.texashealth.org/job/21828996/manager-perioperative-business-operations-arlington-tx)).

| Role | Sits in | Owns | Main tools and channels | Hands the case to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Practice surgical coordinator | Surgeon's office or clinic | The decision-to-surgery checklist: orders, prior auth, clearances, implants, patient instructions, 48-hour confirmation | Practice EHR, payer portals and phone, fax, hospital portal or EpicCare Link | Central scheduler (request), patient, vendor rep |
| Central OR scheduler | Hospital or system scheduling office | Validating, sizing and placing the case; issuing the case number; confirming back | Epic OpTime or another perioperative system, phone, fax, email | Next-day huddle and OR desk (schedule), office (confirmation) |
| OR desk, charge RN and AIC | OR control desk | Today's board: add-ons, bumps, late rooms, final sequence | Face to face, phone, whiteboard or status board | Rooms, PACU, inpatient floors |
| Anesthesia scheduling coordinator | Anesthesia department | Provider-to-room assignments | QGenda, email | Rooms and the AIC |
| ASC scheduler | Surgery center | Placement plus release chasing and favors | Email, phone | Center staff |
| Perioperative business manager | Perioperative leadership | Block allocation and utilization reporting | Reports, block committee | Surgeons' offices (use it or release it) |

Read together, these postings imply a six-step handoff chain. The office collects the decision, orders, authorization and clearances and submits a request; the central scheduler validates, places and confirms it; the next-day huddle finalizes the schedule; anesthesia maps providers onto it; the OR desk and AIC absorb same-day changes; and the business manager reviews utilization afterward. **Each handoff means someone re-reads the same case, often in a different system.** Hospital policies formalize the same sequence. HonorHealth's fax form calls itself a booking request rather than a pre-op order and is labeled a downtime form, so electronic entry is the normal route ([HonorHealth form](https://www.honorhealth.com/sites/default/files/2025-10/surgery-scheduling-fax-request-form.pdf)). At placement, UTMC assigns case time from the surgeon's own history and requires AIC approval for extensions ([UTMC policy 3364-100-53-27](https://www.utoledo.edu/policies/utmc/administrative/pdfs/3364-100-53-27.pdf)). Saint Alphonsus issues the case number at confirmation ([Saint Alphonsus form](https://saintalphonsus.org/sites/default/files/hg_features/mercury_standard_layout/9939b69fdb35876f43bc892c7805e777.pdf)). Readiness, schedule close and the day of surgery follow, as the next sections describe.

Which coordinator does the take-home mean? Indeed listed **1,528** "Surgical Scheduling Coordinator" openings on June 1, 2026 ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-Surgical-Scheduling-Coordinator-jobs.html)), and the title spans both sides of the practice–hospital line. The choice matters for design. A practice-side agent must handle insurance and patient preparation. A hospital-side agent must handle block rules, room capabilities and the board.

Friction between practice and hospital concentrates at three seams. The first is intake completeness: Emory warns that an incomplete form means the case will not be scheduled ([Emory form](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/-/media/Project/EH/Emory/ui/pdfs/south/wrp-or-scheduling-form-05-2026.pdf)). The second is the gap between the office's duration and equipment estimates and the hospital's history-based numbers. The third is late readiness, where clearance and authorization are still open at the 48–72 hour mark.

## A scheduler's day is queue management across four overlapping puzzles

No US time-and-motion study of OR schedulers turned up, so the day has to be pieced together from postings, vendor descriptions and anecdote.

**Caseload.** A busy orthopedic surgeon does **8–15 cases a week**, each confirmed about 48 hours out ([Staffing for Doctors](https://www.staffingfordoctors.com/practice-types/orthopedics/roles/surgical-coordinator), vendor). The AAOS census puts full-time orthopaedists at about **32 procedures a month** ([AAOS 2018 census](https://www.aaos.org/globalassets/quality-and-practice-resources/census/2018-census.pdf)). Coverage is thin. Anonymous 2020 reviewers describe being the only scheduler for four doctors, or one full-time scheduler serving eleven ([Payscale reviews](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Surgery_Scheduler/Reviews/Page-5)). Combining these unrelated figures suggests a practice coordinator carries something like **30 to over 100 active cases** at different stages of readiness. That is an estimate, not a measurement, but it explains why the job feels like managing a queue rather than a calendar.

Each of those cases is heavy. A faxed or emailed booking carries about **70 data elements** that must be tracked and kept current until surgery ([PreferredMD](https://preferredmd.io/blog/what_do_physicians_expect_from_surgical_scheduling), vendor), and a fully engaged scheduler can exchange **more than 40 messages per case** with assistants, OR managers, vendors, anesthesia and authorization teams ([Becker's Spine Review](https://www.beckersspine.com/orthopedic-spine-practices-improving-profits/7-common-causes-of-inefficient-orthopedic-and-spine-case-scheduling/)). The largest single sink is simply finding out whether cases are ready. Surgimate claims coordinators spend **up to 40% of the day** on it, calling payers and digging through files, and frames this as a tax on vigilance, though the figure is unsourced ([Surgimate](https://www.surgimate.com/blog/the-operational-visibility-gap/), vendor). Payer verification calls can hold staff for up to two hours ([PreferredMD](https://preferredmd.io/blog/what_do_physicians_expect_from_surgical_scheduling)), and AMA's 2024 survey, as cited by a revenue-cycle vendor, puts practices at about **39 prior authorizations and 13 staff and physician hours a week** ([Strategic Practice Solutions](https://spsrcm.com/orthopedic-prior-authorizations/)).

The hospital side runs on a predictable daily shape. Mornings go to today's add-ons, cancellations, patient transport and vendor check-ins, handled alongside the charge nurse ([AHN posting](https://zapply.jobs/jobs/c12f5790-9336-4d9c-88d8-d8d805fc7601/)). Midday absorbs new requests and changes. In the afternoon a huddle approves tomorrow's schedule and the office confirms case order back to surgeons' offices ([HSS listing](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Surgery-Scheduler-Coordinator/--in-New-York)), and after hours a separate desk takes over ([BILH posting](https://jobs.bilh.org/jobs/perioperative-ops-assistant-boston-ma-jr98421/)). The anesthesia scheduler's day is a rolling rebuild of provider assignments, weighing specialty, benefit time, special requests, swaps and call status ([BWH posting](https://app.joinhandshake.com/jobs/4872728/share_preview)).

Experienced schedulers carry a private model of every surgeon and room, and it decomposes into **four overlapping puzzles**. The first is *time*: one surgery center's quality review found a group of surgeons who consistently underestimated case length, so schedulers were told to pad their cases, and habitually late surgeons get later slots ([Becker's Spine](https://www.beckersspine.com/orthopedic-spine-practices-improving-profits/8-tips-for-scheduling-at-orthopedic-surgery-centers/), older). The second is *space and equipment*: which room has the robot or C-arm, which is closed, and whether trays will be back from SPD. After CHI Health Lakeside adopted a capacity tool, staff no longer had to memorize room closures ([LeanTaaS](https://leantaas.com/blog/centralized-surgical-scheduling-an-innovative-model-to-streamline-health-systems-address-case-backlogs/), vendor). The third is *people*, meaning anesthesia coverage and specialty teams. The fourth is *politics*. When a request needs another block holder to shift, an administrator calls that surgeon to ask a favor; in one account the business office manager takes care not to ask any one surgeon too often, and surgeons who release time get extra attention from staff ([Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/asc-news/8-points-about-scheduling-cases-in-an-asc/)). The first three puzzles can be modeled from data. The fourth is relational. A vendor notes that experienced surgeons latch onto great schedulers and dread their vacations ([LeanTaaS](https://surgical-clinics.leantaas.com/your-surgery-scheduler-one-of-your-most-overlooked-assets/), vendor). That shows how much of this model lives in one person's head.

The toolset compounds the burden. Schedulers juggle the hospital perioperative system (such as Epic OpTime), the practice EHR, payer portals and phone lines, fax (still the default for orders and clearances), email, spreadsheets, sticky notes, QGenda for anesthesia staffing, and the OR whiteboard, which a 2024 qualitative study of 11 anesthesia clinical directors examined as the tool that still runs perioperative operations ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950553424000108)). One cataract-practice scheduler complained of "wasting more than 5-10 minutes putting surgeries in 4 different places" ([Payscale review, 2020](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Surgery_Scheduler/Reviews/Page-2)). A US patent traces the duplication to disconnected systems: the clinic EMR, the clinic practice-management system, the surgical facility's system and scanning software ([USPTO 12502075](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12502075)). Structured electronic submission helps without eliminating the problem. At Woman's Hospital, electronic case submission averaged **3 minutes 6 seconds** per case, 12% faster than phoning it in ([LeanTaaS case study booklet](https://leantaas.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/iQueue-for-OR-Case-Study-Booklet.pdf), vendor). Fax survives because it is the lowest common denominator across independent practices, payers and hospitals, so an agent design must assume some inputs arrive as images.

In their own words, schedulers describe a job that is overloaded and underseen. In anonymous 2020 reviews, the best first-person material found, a cataract-practice scheduler said the work had tripled without communication, that they felt expected to produce surgeries like a machine, and that they were reprimanded for missing a daily surgery quota. A Cincinnati scheduler, the only one in the specialty, described doctors piling on work with no extra pay, and a Memphis reviewer cited poor communication and feeling unvalued while calling the job rewarding when patients did well ([Payscale reviews](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Surgery_Scheduler/Reviews/Page-2)). Pay in postings runs about **$18–40 an hour** ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Perioperative-Scheduler)). A vendor says new schedulers need days or weeks for basic proficiency and years for excellence ([LeanTaaS](https://surgical-clinics.leantaas.com/7-best-surgery-scheduling-practices/), vendor). Turnover forces repeated retraining, and training on phone-based insurance verification can leave mentor and new hire on hold for an hour before the lesson begins ([PreferredMD](https://preferredmd.io/blog/what_do_physicians_expect_from_surgical_scheduling)). No scheduler-specific turnover rate was found.

The complaints sort into three groups: **chasing** missing information; **re-keying and re-broadcasting**, where one change means many calls and faxes; and **absorbing other people's variance**, from underestimated durations to late surgeons, add-ons and anesthesia call-outs. An agent can take on the first two directly. On the third it should advise rather than decide. The tone of the reviews also argues for positioning the agent as the scheduler's assistant that removes drudgery. A tool that bypasses schedulers or reports on their speed would likely meet resistance. That is an inference, not a measured finding.

## Half of all ORs are still changing two days before surgery

### The practice manages a dependency graph, not a calendar

A vendor's orthopedic checklist shows the practice side of a case. After the decision to operate, the coordinator books hospital or ASC block time with the H&P inside its 30-day window, schedules pre-op clearance and confirms it is uploaded, chases labs (CBC, CMP, INR, type and screen), and secures prior authorization for both the procedure and the implants. The coordinator then sends implant sizes to the rep, confirms trays are at the facility, coordinates Mako, ROSA or navigation equipment, sends pre-op instructions, coordinates anesthesia, confirms 48 hours out and manages post-op follow-up ([Staffing for Doctors](https://www.staffingfordoctors.com/practice-types/orthopedics/roles/surgical-coordinator), vendor).

Hospitals layer hard deadlines on top. At UTMC, the pre-op evaluation falls 3–30 business days out, chart documentation is due **72 hours** ahead, financial clearance by **noon the day before**, and vendor implants must be in SPD **48 hours** ahead; missing any of these can postpone the case or remove it from the schedule ([UTMC](https://www.utoledo.edu/policies/utmc/administrative/pdfs/3364-100-53-27.pdf)). St. Charles Bend requires an approved or pending prior-authorization number to book within **14 days** of surgery, with the date inside the authorization's valid range. A change in resources that needs new authorization removes the case from the schedule until it arrives, and a case needing a fast-track supply item is not scheduled until the item is approved ([St. Charles 3899](http://www.stcharleshealthcare.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Surgery%20Scheduling/2024/SurgeryProceduralScheduleManagementBend-Policy3899.pdf)). UTMB asks the surgeon's office to notify the OR and Purchasing about loaner needs when the case is scheduled. Loaner sets are due in SPD 48 hours ahead, or three business days ahead if the set is new to the hospital ([UTMB](https://www.utmb.edu/policies_and_procedures/20038472)). HealthTrust notes that sets are supposed to arrive at least a day early but often do not, which causes delays and cancellations ([HealthTrust](https://healthtrustpg.com/thesource/clinical-connection/quality-improvement/reducing-unnecessary-cost-risk-in-the-or/)).

**The key insight for a newcomer is that the OR slot is only one node in a graph.** Authorization hangs off it by CPT code, site and date range; clearance and labs by a window counted back from the surgery date; implants by date and facility; and patient instructions by date and arrival time. A change to date, CPT, site or patient class therefore invalidates everything downstream. HonorHealth tells offices that changing location, patient class or CPT means calling the insurer to update the authorization ([HonorHealth form](https://www.honorhealth.com/sites/default/files/2025-10/surgery-scheduling-fax-request-form.pdf)). A practice vendor describes one date change as a round of calls and faxes to every party, a fresh confirmation with the patient, and re-coordination with vendors and the facility ([PreferredMD](https://preferredmd.io/blog/what_do_physicians_expect_from_surgical_scheduling)). Surgimate draws the conclusion that a booked case is not a ready case ([Surgimate](https://www.surgimate.com/blog/the-three-moments-when-surgical-coordination-usually-fails/), vendor).

### Churn, denials and cancellations crowd into the final 72 hours

The best quantitative picture of schedule churn comes from Dexter and colleagues, who rebuilt each date's OR schedule from timestamped audit tables at a US academic hospital. **24.1%** of OR-dates had at least one add-on, **51.5%** of ORs had their last case scheduled or changed within two working days of surgery, and once allocated time was full, extra cases arrived with a median lead time of 2.2 workdays ([Dexter et al., Anesth Analg 2012](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Descriptive-study-of-case-scheduling-and/9983806274002771)). The authors urged anesthesia groups to coordinate with the scheduling office during those final two days.

A follow-up study found that inpatients dominate cancelled time ([Dexter et al., Anesth Analg 2014](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Relative-influence-on-total-cancelled-operating/9983806374002771)). At one academic hospital they made up **22.3% of scheduled minutes but 70% of cancelled minutes**, and about 54% of inpatient cancelled minutes came from cases scheduled within a workday of surgery. Outpatient cancellation could be held to about 2% or lower with phone-based preoperative evaluation.

Reported cancellation rates depend heavily on what window and population are measured.

| Source | Population | Rate | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| [VA, 2006 data](https://aemqa.stanfordhealthcare.org/publications/414/414224.html) (abstract summary) | All scheduled cases, 123 facilities | **12.4%** | Patient 35%, work-up or medical change 28%, facility 20%, surgeon 8%, anesthesia 1% |
| [Albany Medical Center, 2022–23](https://www.amc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/06/Outlook-Day-of-Surgery-Cancellations-for-Albany-Medical-Center-Main-Campus-A-Retrospective-Investigation.pdf) | Adult elective outpatients, day of surgery | **1.1%** | 71% patient-related; within those, 44% not fit, 11% no-show, 14% did not follow pre-op instructions |
| [Qventus eBook](https://www.qventus.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Qventus-eBook-35MProblem-SurgicalLeakage-1.pdf) (vendor) | One large system | **15%** within 72 hours | Only about half performed within 90 days |

The VA's facility codes include broken or unavailable equipment, unavailable implants, no bed, scheduling errors, staff shortages and emergencies displacing electives ([VA codes table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4184311/table/T7)). The Qventus leakage claim conflicts with a University of Iowa finding that 90% of cancelled cases later had the same or a similar procedure at the same hospital ([University of Iowa](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Rescheduling-of-previously-cancelled-surgical-cases/9983806379402771), snippet). How much cancelled volume really leaks is therefore contested.

Prior authorization is the late trigger that matters most for joints and spine. In a 2016 ISASS survey, spine surgeons estimated that 24.6% of cases were denied during pre-authorization and that **58.5% of denials arrived no more than three days before surgery** ([ISASS](https://www.isass.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/PreAuth-Survey-Press-Release.pdf)). In a single-institution AAOS 2026 abstract, 105 of 584 scheduled primary total joint arthroplasties (**17.9%**) were cancelled in a 50-day window, with insurance problems the top cause at 36.2% ([AAOS 2026 abstract](https://index.mirasmart.com/AAOS2026/PDFfiles/AAOS2026-005559.pdf)). Booking errors are rare but costly. A summary of a study covering more than 17,000 surgeries found a 0.86% booking error rate. Wrong side accounted for 36% of those errors and wrong approach for 17%, and most were caught only in holding or the OR ([Patient Safety Solutions](https://www.patientsafetysolutions.com/docs/October_30_2012_Surgical_Scheduling_Errors.htm)).

Taken together, schedule close, chart and implant deadlines, late denials and add-ons all collide between **five days out and the day of surgery**, not at the moment of booking. Facility-side causes and late payer denials respond to better process. Patient fitness responds to earlier readiness work, not scheduling.

### Readiness outreach at 48 to 72 hours is where measured gains appear

Every intervention that has moved cancellation rates reaches out before the deadline. At an ASC, scripted nurse calls three business days before surgery, covering no-shows, NPO and transport, cut daily cancellations by **53%** (Haufler & Harrington, as cited by [LeanTaaS](https://leantaas.com/blog/preoperative-readiness/)). At Kaiser Permanente Sacramento, email or phone outreach 72 hours ahead cut preventable same-day cancellations from **27 to 13** in a 13-week pilot ([KP NCAL Nursing Annual Report 2025](https://nursingncal.kaiserpermanente.org/annual-report-2025/new-knowledge-innovation-improvements/reducing-same-day-surgical-cancellations-a-pre-op-pacu-process-improvement-initiative/)). OHSU's AI-enabled outreach with human escalation reportedly cut targeted late cancellations by more than 50%, according to a sponsored conference session ([Becker's session](https://conferences.beckershospitalreview.com/transform-hospital-operations-sept-2026/session/4262232/50-percent-+-reduction-in-late-cancellations-how-ohsu-scaled-coordinated-case-planning-to-prepare-patients-earlier)). Structure matters more than format. At one academic hospital, day-of cancellations ran 0.48% after comprehensive assessment, 0.60% after nurse phone screening and **2.02% with no structured assessment** (Olson & Dhakal, as cited by [LeanTaaS](https://leantaas.com/blog/preoperative-readiness/)). Meanwhile, Dexter found that more in-person preoperative clinic visits did not lower outpatient cancelled minutes ([Dexter et al. 2014](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Relative-influence-on-total-cancelled-operating/9983806374002771)).

Qventus describes Southern Health System's baseline as hours of faxing, unanswered calls and chart combing; after deployment, it reports PAT capacity rising from **1.8 to 2.8 screenings per hour**, cancellations falling from about 4.9% to 3.5%, and fax processing time falling 60% ([Qventus case study](https://www.qventus.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Qventus-Southern-health-system-PCC-Case-Study.pdf), vendor). LeanTaaS puts the root cause simply: readiness gaps surface at check-in when nobody owns the next step ([LeanTaaS](https://leantaas.com/blog/preoperative-readiness/), vendor). All of these results are self-reported quality-improvement or vendor data. **No independent evaluation of an AI agent in OR scheduling or readiness was found.**

### Late changes ripple into sterile processing and the case cart

SPD builds each case cart from the surgeon's preference card for the booked procedure, so booking accuracy and card accuracy together decide whether the right trays arrive. AORN gives the example of a skin-graft mesher: it was a loaner that was missing from the card, and the patient had to return the next day ([AORN](https://aorn.org/article/why-do-i-need-preference-cards)). The failure rates are material. OR-reported tray defects affected about **5% of 41,799 cases**, with missing instruments the most common assembly defect ([BMJ Quality & Safety](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7979531)). Direct observation in seven pediatric ORs found instrument errors in 42.7% of inpatient cases, and over half of the affected cases were delayed ([BMC Surgery](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12893-024-02407-1), snippet). Fixing the cards pays: at Stanford, an algorithm flagged inaccurate cards, nurses revised 309 of them, and direct supply cost per case fell 8.38% in the intervention group while rising 13.21% in the control group ([Stanford/JAMIA](https://stanfordhealthcare.org/publications/804/804134.html)). St. Charles also derives turnover from the procedure card and duration from provider-specific EHR averages ([St. Charles 3899](http://www.stcharleshealthcare.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Surgery%20Scheduling/2024/SurgeryProceduralScheduleManagementBend-Policy3899.pdf)). The booked procedure therefore drives the cart, the duration and the turnover all at once. **Any late change to procedure, side, approach, surgeon or date after the cart is picked has to reach SPD and the vendor**, and an agent can enforce that notification.

| When | Stage | Owner | Hard deadline (example) | Typical failure | Where an agent helps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-30 to T-14 days | Request and placement | Office → central scheduler | Complete form or no booking (Emory) | Missing fields, wrong laterality, auth not started | Extract request, check completeness, open the readiness graph |
| T-14 to T-5 days | Auth, PAT, clearances, vendor | Office, payer, PAT, vendor, purchasing | Auth on file to book inside 14 days (St. Charles) | Late denial, missing records, loaner never requested | Chase named owners with deadlines; escalate |
| T-5 to T-2 days | Release and close | Scheduling, anesthesia | Close 5 PM two business days out (St. Charles) or 2 PM the day before (UTMC) | Half of ORs still changing; add-ons; late inpatient adds | Push changes to SPD, vendor, anesthesia, PAT and patient |
| T-72 to T-48 hours | Readiness deadlines | PAT, SPD, financial clearance | Chart at 72 h; implants in SPD at 48 h (UTMC) | Chart incomplete, tray missing, denial arrives | At-risk list for the scheduler; patient outreach |
| T-1 day | Confirmation | Scheduling → office and patient | Clearance by noon (UTMC) | Wrong arrival time; cart picked for the wrong variant | Automated confirmations; answer "is my case on?" |
| Day of surgery | Execution | Charge RN, AIC, OR desk | Urgency-class start targets | Late rooms, bumps, missing instruments, consent and site-marking delays | Real-time status; compute options; draft notifications |

## The day of surgery runs on 20-second conversations and negotiated rules

The best measurement of the OR desk is old but unusually detailed. Moss and Xiao observed charge nurses in four OR suites at three tertiary hospitals over 17 days and logged **2,074 communication episodes at 32 to 74 an hour** ([Moss & Xiao, JONA 2004](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Moss-JONA04.pdf)). Equipment coordination (39%) and patient preparedness (26%) dominated; staffing was 19%, room assignment 11%, and scheduling or rescheduling only **6%**, except in a trauma suite, where scheduling exceeded 32%. Some 69% of episodes were face to face, 18% by phone and 7% by intercom, and email and voicemail were never used. The median episode lasted 20 seconds or less and 93% lasted under a minute, though scheduling conversations ran longer because they often involved negotiation. The authors concluded that automated status displays and short messages could absorb much of the preparedness and equipment querying. Channel shares have surely shifted since secure chat arrived, but the pattern holds: before surgery the network is asynchronous and fax-heavy, and on the day it is synchronous, measured in seconds, and face to face.

Day-of decisions follow explicit rules, but applying them involves negotiation. St. Charles Bend's policy is the most complete example found ([St. Charles 3899](http://www.stcharleshealthcare.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Surgery%20Scheduling/2024/SurgeryProceduralScheduleManagementBend-Policy3899.pdf)). After close, add-ons are phoned to the charge RN and submitted electronically; they are accepted on acuity and available resources, urgency questions go to the AIC, and none are accepted more than 24 hours ahead. Its urgency classes are emergency within 60 minutes, emergent trauma within 15 and urgent within 24 hours. When no suite is free, the charge nurse may consult the AIC and the providers about bumping a case, the surgeon with the emergency must personally tell the surgeon being bumped, and the AIC owns final sequence. The charge RN and AIC watch for late rooms all day, and a late room may start an elective case only if **two-thirds of it can finish before the block ends and the overrun stays under 45 minutes**; otherwise the case goes to the add-on queue or is rescheduled. Disputes escalate from provider to provider, then to the AIC, then to the perioperative manager on call. Sequencing puts same-day discharges and patients with a history of malignant hyperthermia first and isolation cases last.

Other hospitals vary the pattern. UTMC treats start times after the first case as estimates and requires AIC approval for add-ons ([UTMC](https://www.utoledo.edu/policies/utmc/administrative/pdfs/3364-100-53-27.pdf)). Hendrick Health requires on-call anesthesia approval for cases that will not finish by 19:00 and sends bump disputes to the Chief of Surgery ([Hendrick policy](https://www.hendrickhealth.org/documents/content/Scheduling-of-Patients-for-Surgery-4-9164.pdf), snippet). St. Charles Redmond expects providers to bump their own partner or specialty first ([Redmond policy](https://WWW.STCHARLESHEALTHCARE.ORG/sites/default/files/Documents/Surgery%20Scheduling/2024/SurgeryProceduralScheduleManagementRedmond-Policy2212.pdf), snippet). An OR Manager framework escalates urgency over time, so a four-hour case that has waited three hours becomes a one-hour case ([OR Manager](https://www.ormanager.com/wp-content/uploads/pdfx/ORMVol26No7ManagingUrgentCases.pdf), snippet).

Room moves help the cases moved but barely change the suite. Moving late-running cases cut their tardiness by 50–70% but cut suite-wide tardiness only 6–9%, because few cases moved ([Dexter group](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Reducing-tardiness-from-scheduled-start-times/9983806372702771), snippet). First-case delays came mainly from completing consent, site marking and the surgical briefing ([ACHE 2025 poster](https://www.ache.org/-/media/ache/learning-center/research/2025-poster-presentation-posters/poster-18.pdf), snippet). Flip rooms appeared on about 14% of days in one consultancy's sample ([Becker's ASC](https://beckersasc.com/anesthesia/how-flip-rooms-affect-anesthesia-practice-productivity-4-things-to-know.html), snippet).

The question offices and families ask most is whether the case is on, and when. Today the OR desk answers it by phone. In a 2024 feasibility study of an SMS messenger for families, **99% of 173 messages were read on mobile devices**, and staff named the absence of extra workload as a key requirement ([Digital Health 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/20552076241288760), snippet).

Every policy names the AIC and charge RN as the decision-makers. The agent's role is to supply status, compute the options (which room finishes first, whether a case passes the two-thirds rule) and draft the notifications to the bumped surgeon, the office, the patient and PACU.

## Setting, room and specialty change parameters far more than logic

### Settings mostly decide who is eligible and how predictable the day is

**ASCs** take only cases that Medicare and the facility consider safe to discharge the same day. Through 2025, a covered ASC procedure had to pose no significant safety risk and could not require active monitoring at midnight. Procedures involving extensive blood loss, major vessels or emergent status were excluded ([42 CFR 416.166](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-416/subpart-F/section-416.166)).

**The site-of-service boundary moves every year.** Total knee arthroplasty left Medicare's Inpatient-Only list in 2018 ([MedBridge](https://www.medbridge.com/blog/the-inpatient-only-list-is-almost-gone-what-the-proposed-2027-outpatient-rule-means-for-where-you-operate)). In 2026 CMS began a three-year phase-out of the whole list, removing 285 mostly musculoskeletal procedures (Optum counts 271), and added about 560 codes to the ASC list, including revision hip and knee arthroplasty ([AAHKS summary](https://www.aahks.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/AAHKS-Summary-2026-Medicare-OPPS-ASC-Final-Rules.pdf); [Optum](https://www.optumcoding.com/upload/docs/OPPS_ASC_2026_AAPC%20presentation.pdf); [McDermott+](https://www.mcdermottplus.com/?p=159014)). Another 637 removals are proposed for 2027 but are not final ([MedBridge](https://www.medbridge.com/blog/the-inpatient-only-list-is-almost-gone-what-the-proposed-2027-outpatient-rule-means-for-where-you-operate)). **Patient selection** is set by facilities and payers, not by a national rule. Blue Shield of California treats ASA III or higher as high anesthetic risk, with exceptions, and flags BMI over 40 combined with problems such as severe sleep apnea ([Blue Shield CA](https://blueshieldca.com/content/dam/bsca/en/provider/docs/medical-policies/Ambulatory-Surg-Center.pdf)). Among 3,444 joint-replacement patients, **BMI over 40 was the most common reason for ASC ineligibility (32.6%)** ([Becker's ASC](https://beckersasc.com/orthopedics-tjr/4-key-points-on-appropriate-patient-selection-for-total-joint-replacement-in-ascs.html)).

**Throughput.** ASC procedures ran **31.8 minutes shorter** than hospital procedures ([Munnich & Parente](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/procedures-take-less-time-at-ambulatory-surgery-centers-keeping-c-2/)). Another study found total perioperative time 39% shorter but could not separate efficiency from patient selection ([PMC3792562](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3792562)).

**Procedural areas** run their own slot-based schedules, often in separate EHR modules. Epic's cardiology module, for example, places emergent STEMI cases on its own board ([University of Iowa Epic support](https://epicsupport.sites.uiowa.edu/epic-resources/cupid)). When one US cath lab reserved one or two of its four labs for urgent work, elective bumping stopped and **urgent waits fell 75%** within six months ([Cath Lab Digest](https://hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/cathlab/articles/doing-more-less-overtime-improving-patient-flow-through-cath-lab)).

A setting is therefore an eligibility gate plus a parameter set. The ASC procedure list and patient criteria belong in data with effective dates.

### Rooms differ in capability, non-operative time and whether they are bookable at all

**Robotic rooms** carry longer non-operative time. A 2010 series found a median robot setup of 22 minutes and a median docking of 10 ([Université de Genève](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:21022)). Two single-center turnover studies report a **median of 67 minutes and a mean of 72 minutes**, against UTMC's 30-minute main-OR target ([PubMed 35220451](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35220451/); [PubMed 31933120](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31933120/)). Both were attributed from search results without confirming the robotic platform, so verify them before quoting. St. Charles auto-releases robotics blocks at 14 days instead of 7 ([St. Charles 9579](https://WWW.STCHARLESHEALTHCARE.ORG/sites/default/files/Documents/Surgery%20Scheduling/2024/SurgicalProceduralBlockManagementRedmond-Policy9579.pdf)).

**Hybrid ORs** pair fixed angiography with a full OR. They host procedures such as TAVR, with a standby surgeon and heart-lung machine ([ACC](https://acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2015/04/27/11/05/clinical-advances-with-hybrid-surgical-technologies)), and are shared among services under heavy demand; one surgeon's hybrid room ran at 80–90% capacity ([DotMed](https://www.dotmed.com/news/story/20488?p_begin=2)).

**Trauma and obstetric rooms** are held capacity, not inventory. A Level I trauma center needs an OR available within **15 minutes** ([AAST](https://www.aast.org/asset/EBB2EE64%2D6F1C%2D4205%2DA99321D990FE6388)). Emergent cesareans follow a 30-minute decision-to-incision expectation, a consensus standard that no outcome data supports ([OBG Management](https://cdn.mdedge.com/files/s3fs-public/Document/September-2017/1910OBGM_Article4.pdf)).

Capability flags, turnover defaults and release horizons are parameters on a generic room model. Held rooms that emergencies consume need different logic.

### Specialties change durations, special staff, beds and urgency mix

Duration variability depends on both specialty and urgency. Across 61,353 University of Iowa cases, 57% of urgent spine cases had prediction errors above the overall median of 22%, against 42% of elective spine cases ([University of Iowa](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Influence-of-procedure-classification-on-process/9983983624902771)). For complex adult spinal deformity, the institution or the surgeon was the strongest predictor of duration ([Duke repository](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/6a2ad177-04a9-4106-a62b-4370f91b62b6/full)). Durations should therefore be modeled per surgeon and procedure, with variability as a parameter.

A handful of specialties change more than parameters. **Cardiac surgery** couples the OR to ICU beds and perfusion: one UK audit found 16.6% of cardiac cases cancelled on the day, citing ICU beds, perfusionists and emergencies ([STS abstract](https://www.sts.org/sites/default/files/documents/Thurs%20A_1210_ABSTRACT%20The%20Cancellation_Jasionowska.pdf)), and scheduling short-stay pediatric cardiac cases early in the week smoothed ICU census and cut cancellations ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frhs.2021.787358)). **Transplant** arrives unscheduled and, in a full schedule, sets off cascading elective cancellations ([University of Auckland](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/handle/2292/31941)). **Ophthalmology** runs two identical rooms in tandem to reach 5–6 cataracts an hour, and surgeons doing fewer than three an hour should not get two rooms ([Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/news-analysis-asc/business-financial-benchmarking/11-ways-to-improve-efficiency-quality-and-profitability-in-ophthalmology.html)). **Pediatrics** adds an age-based staffing match, because ACS-verified Level III centers need a pediatric anesthesiologist, or one with pediatric expertise, to provide or oversee anesthesia for every child aged 2 or younger ([ACS questionnaire](https://ww2.facs.org/media/cmsc0eno/2021_re_csv_prqs_level_iii_v02.pdf)), plus sequencing conventions such as youngest-first from UK and Australian guidance ([Cambridge University Hospitals](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/paediatric-surgery-list-orders-information-for-parents-and-carers/)).

| Service line | What differs for the scheduler | Parameters or logic? |
|---|---|---|
| Orthopedics: joints | Implant and loaner lead times, vendor rep, Mako/ROSA robot, ASC eligibility (BMI, sleep apnea), same-day versus overnight stay, flip rooms for high-volume surgeons | Parameters; the richest standard profile |
| Orthopedics: spine | Durations from 71 to 378 minutes; urgent cases far less predictable; IONM supplied by an outside vendor ([NuVasive](https://jobs.dukecapitalpartners.duke.edu/companies/nuvasive/jobs/48489353-ncs-operations-coordinator)) | Parameters, plus a step to confirm the external resource |
| Orthopedic trauma | Dedicated room; HonorHealth releases the ortho-trauma block at 7 a.m. on the day | Logic: held capacity released on the day |
| Cardiac surgery | Perfusion, cardiac anesthesia, an ICU bed required; weekday smoothing | Logic: separate module that reserves OR and ICU bed together |
| Transplant | Unscheduled start; team availability by shift; displaces electives | Logic: separate module for unscheduled arrival and preemption |
| Ophthalmology | Very short cases; tandem rooms gated by throughput | Generic parallel-room strategy plus an eligibility threshold |
| Pediatrics (cross-cutting) | Pediatric anesthesiologist for age 2 or under; youngest or diabetic patients first; high relative variability for short cases ([UCLA](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2nw3r72h)) | Parameters in a generic sequencing-rules engine, plus a staffing match |
| Trauma, acute care surgery, emergent OB, STEMI | Response-time standards; held rooms or labs | Logic: separate reserve-and-preempt module |

Urology, ENT, plastics, vascular and general surgery appear to fit the standard parameterized profile. This research found no sourced scheduling specifics for them, so their settings are assumptions to validate with a client.

### Emergencies are a separate path, and protected capacity is a structural choice

Urgency classes are defined hospital by hospital; this research found no US national standard. Hospitals protect elective schedules structurally: with dedicated non-elective rooms (Mayo simulated dedicated, non-elective and versatile room designs after finding emergencies badly disrupted electives ([WSC 2014](https://informs-sim.org/wsc14papers/includes/files/122.pdf))), with reserved labs like the cath lab above, or with reservation models that cap how many electives to accept given expected emergency demand ([Gerchak et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.42.3.321)). The elective path is a constraint-satisfying search for a slot. The urgent path is triage-and-notify: capture the class the surgeon states, the procedure and the required capabilities; notify the AIC; show which rooms could take the case and which electives would be affected; and record the human decision.

### Four behaviors need their own logic; everything else is configuration

Nearly all the variation above fits as data on one constraint model: capabilities, required roles, external confirmations, lead times, duration distributions, turnover, post-op bed type, eligibility lists, sequencing preferences and release horizons. Control flow changes genuinely in only four places: **reserve-and-preempt** for unscheduled arrivals (trauma, transplant, emergent OB, STEMI); **bed-coupled booking** for cardiac, transplant and ICU-heavy neurosurgery; **parallel-room throughput** for tandem and flip rooms; and **site-of-service gating** across ASC, outpatient department and main OR. The last two can be built as generic, configurable components, so only the first two need modules of their own. This split is a synthesis of the cited constraints; no industry taxonomy for it was found.

## Language models earn their keep at the edges of the workflow

### Chat wins on stating intent, and the grid wins on confidence

Human-computer interaction research supports neither "chat replaces the GUI" nor "chat is a gimmick." In DirectGPT (CHI 2024), a direct-manipulation layer over an LLM, where the user selects an object and then issues a command, made users **about 50% faster** than plain chat with 50% fewer prompts that were 72% shorter ([DirectGPT](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3613904.3642462)). In a 2026 preprint comparing a chatbot with a dashboard (20 participants), the chatbot lowered workload and time for retrieval, yet **when confidence was the criterion, all 18 decisive responses favored the dashboard**, and participants combined the two unprompted ([arXiv 2605.31224](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31224)). A Stanford preprint found task-specific generated UIs beat chat 87.5% of the time on business and operations queries but added load on simple how-to questions ([arXiv 2508.19227](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19227v2)), and NN/g warns that interfaces which keep regenerating force users to relearn them ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/generative-ui/)).

Jakob Nielsen argues that a usable intent has three parts: the outcome, the constraints, and a **delegation boundary** stating what the system may do itself versus only prepare. He recommends progressive delegation, starting with draft-and-confirm and widening authority as reliability is shown ([Nielsen](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/intent-ux?open=false), opinion). His earlier essay on the articulation barrier warns that prompt-only interfaces favor skilled writers ([Nielsen](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/prompt-driven-ai-ux-hurts-usability), opinion). Expert central schedulers who know the vocabulary will cope. Occasional office users need suggested prompts and parameterized cards.

A request like "180 minutes with the robot, sometime in the next three weeks, not Fridays" is ideal conversational input. The answer should come back as scannable option cards rather than prose, and the schedule board should stay a stable, conventional grid with AI annotations attached.

### LLMs read and write well but schedule poorly

The documented strengths lie in unstructured input and repetitive conversation. On 200 synthetic colorectal referral fax packets, an open-weight pipeline extracted labs with 98.9% exact accuracy and diagnoses with 100%, with no fabricated facts, though synthetic data means real-world confirmation is still needed ([medRxiv preprint](https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.64898/2026.10.03.26364667)). Infinitus's benefits-verification voice agents offset the work of more than 100 FTEs at Cencora ([Infinitus](https://infinitus.ai/case-studies/cencora-patient-services/), vendor). The documented weaknesses lie in constraint satisfaction, policy adherence and trust. On DeepMind's NATURAL PLAN benchmark, even the best long-context setting solved **only about 50% of calendar-scheduling tasks** ([NATURAL PLAN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04520)). On τ-bench, agents completed fewer than half of policy-bound booking tasks, and pass^8 fell below 25% ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/benchmarking-ai-agents)). Fluent explanations raise perceived credibility among users who cannot check them ([arXiv 2606.24370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24370)), and Bainbridge warned decades ago that automating the routine leaves humans with the rarest, hardest cases while eroding their skills ([Bainbridge 1983](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8)). Anthropic's guidance is to prefer predefined workflows when the steps are predictable and to reserve agents for open-ended problems ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). In OR scheduling that means a clean division of labor: **the LLM interprets and communicates, and deterministic code decides what is feasible.**

### Production deployments automate the happy path and hand off the rest

Healthcare deployments that report results share an asymmetric design: the agent finishes routine cases on its own and hands exceptions to people. At MUSC Health, Notable's agents complete about **40% of prior authorizations with no human involvement**, turning roughly 30 minutes of manual work into about one minute; approvals are written into Epic and denials go to a person ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/ai/prior-authorizations-patient-check-ins-musc-healths-ai-agents.html)). The strongest peer-reviewed staff-relief signal comes from ambient scribes, AI that removes work in the background with human review rather than adding a chat surface; among 263 clinicians, burnout fell from **51.9% to 38.8%** in a pre-post study without a control group ([Yale/JAMA Network Open](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/ai-scribes-reduce-physician-burnout-return-focus-to-the-patient/)). Qventus's Perioperative Care Coordination teammates already place and take calls, text patients and act on follow-ups in the EHR, with up to 40% fewer cancellations claimed ([Qventus PCC launch](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-launches-perioperative-care-coordination-solution-to-alleviate-the-administrative-burden-on-clinicians-reduce-unnecessary-surgery-cancellations-and-optimize-patients-pre-and-post-surgery/), vendor). Its Surgical Growth line sells a Clinic Scheduler Assistant with a **22-minute median booking turnaround**, a Document Assistant for scheduling faxes, and bi-directional write-back ([Qventus, May 2025](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-ai-teammates-transform-the-perioperative-care-journey-driving-strategic-surgical-growth-and-winning-several-new-clients-and-enterprise-wide-expansions/), vendor). Together these point to background agents for intake, chasing and monitoring as the main value. Chat serves as the command line and the place where exceptions surface.

### Trust depends on legibility, integration and a path to a human

In AMA's survey of 2024 data, physicians' top conditions for adopting AI were a feedback channel (88%), data privacy assurances (87%) and EHR integration (84%) ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/physician-ai-sentiment-report.pdf)). A review of 37 nursing studies found a consistent preference for AI that supports staff rather than replacing them ([PMC12507812](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12507812/)). Nurses' top worries are loss of human connection and accountability when an algorithm is wrong ([American Nurse](https://www.myamericannurse.com/2025-2026-trends-survey/)). No survey of OR schedulers specifically was found.

Feng and colleagues define five autonomy levels, from operator to observer, and argue that autonomy is a deliberate design choice separate from what a model can do ([arXiv 2506.12469](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12469v1)). Regulation already constrains outbound contact. The FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices fall under the TCPA's rules for artificial voices ([Hunton](https://huntonak.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/fcc-issues-declaratory-ruling-that-tcpa-applies-to-ai-generated-voice-calls)), and California's AB 3030 requires an AI disclaimer and a way to reach a human in generative-AI patient communications about clinical information ([Arent Fox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/california-requires-disclaimers-health-care-providers-ai-generated-patient)). For schedulers, this translates into outreach sent under the coordinator's or team's name with the coordinator copied, any thread available for a human to take over, and every agent action attributed in an audit trail.

## Recommendations: build the booking spine, design for the case's whole life

*Everything in this section is a recommendation synthesized from the evidence above, not a reported finding.*

### Map each task to a modality and a delegation tier

The delegation tiers below follow progressive delegation, from informing to acting autonomously inside a bounded loop.

| Tier | What the agent may do |
|---|---|
| **T0** | Inform: read, summarize, flag |
| **T1** | Draft an action for one-click approval |
| **T2** | Carry out low-risk, reversible, internal actions with undo and a post-action summary |
| **T3** | Run a multi-step outbound loop under a run contract (who, what, how many attempts, deadline), then escalate |
| **Human** | The agent only recommends |

Autonomy should be a dial set per action type and per site. The prototype should ship everything at T1 or below and show where promotion would happen as measured accuracy allows.

| Task | When | Primary modality | What the AI does | Tier | Prototype |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Turn a faxed, phoned or portal request into a structured case request | T-30 to T-14 days | Background agent; source and extracted fields shown side by side | Extract; flag missing and conflicting fields (laterality versus order) | T1, later T2 | Stub: fixture of extracted output feeding the real validators |
| Check completeness and consistency | Intake | Plain automation | Validators decide; the LLM only phrases the ask | Automated | **Build** |
| Site-of-service eligibility (ASC, outpatient department, main OR) | Intake | Deterministic rule check | Explain the configured reasons | T0 | Stub: one rule set |

### Treat most variation as configuration and isolate four behaviors as modules

The architecture that follows from the evidence is one booking engine with many profiles. Each profile is a configuration bundle plus a choice of booking strategy, and it exposes a small interface:

| Method | Returns |
|---|---|
| `eligible(case, site)` | Pass or fail, with reasons |
| `required_resources(case)` | Room capabilities, assets, staff roles, external confirmations and lead times |
| `duration(case)` | A duration distribution |
| `sequencing_prefs(case)` | Soft sequencing rules |
| `post_op(case)` | Bed type |
| `booking_strategy` | `standard`, `bed_coupled`, `tandem` or `reserve_and_preempt` |

The lifecycle adds two more seams. `open_loop(item, owner, channel, run_contract)` handles chasing. `impact(case, proposed_change)` handles change propagation and returns the invalidated items and the notifications to send.

|---|---|---|---|

### Keep the central coordinator and the robotic right knee, with targeted updates

The earlier round of research recommended a central OR scheduling coordinator at a fictional two-campus health system, acting on a request from Dr. Priya Patel's orthopedic office for a **robotic-assisted right total knee arthroplasty**. It froze the clock at Wednesday, October 14, 2026, 09:00, targeted the week of November 2 with synthetic rooms, blocks, surgeons and durations, and specified a six-turn happy path, an alternate path into Urology's unreleased robotics block, a failure path where the slot is lost to a race at commit, an escalation path handing an urgent case to the AIC, and identity and laterality checks that include two patients with the same name. The new evidence supports both the persona and the procedure.

**The central coordinator stays the primary persona.** The assignment's own wording, a coordinator who checks availability and books an operating room, describes the hospital scheduler's specialty: block ownership, room capability and sizing cases from history. Qventus's OR products also save that scheduler's hours. The practice coordinator's dominant work belongs to adjacent products: payer agents, the patient concierge and PCC.

**The office scheduler belongs in the design as a second persona on the same engine.** This mirrors Qventus's Clinic Scheduler Assistant ([Qventus, May 2025](https://www.qventus.com/company/newsroom/qventus-ai-teammates-transform-the-perioperative-care-journey-driving-strategic-surgical-growth-and-winning-several-new-clients-and-enterprise-wide-expansions/)). Its permissions would be scoped: it can request time, see its surgeons' cases and readiness, and see released and open time, but it cannot book into another owner's block or override durations. It is also the main counterparty for outbound chasing and the main source of status questions. Offices today phone schedulers to confirm insurance, equipment and rep notification because they cannot see coordination status ([Whitman Partners](https://whitmanpartners.com/challenges-coordinating-surgical-care/)).

**The robotic right knee still exercises the most generic constraints without needing event-driven logic.** It involves a robot-capable room with a 14-day robotics release horizon, a loaner tray with an SPD deadline and a vendor rep, laterality, history-based duration, and prior authorization, the top cancellation cause in one total-joint series. It now also touches site-of-service gating, because knee replacement left the Inpatient-Only list in 2018 and BMI over 40 is the leading cause of ASC ineligibility.

For a 4–6 hour budget, the sensible order is the mock service and profile configuration first, then the state machine with per-turn extraction, the readiness graph, the alternate and escalation paths, and the eval harness, followed by `get_case_status` and, only if time remains, the change-impact preview. That sequence fully covers what the rubric grades: intent routing, slot capture, backend calls, a happy path and failure paths. Every deferred piece also gets a visible, explainable seam.

## Conclusion

Seen from inside the role, an OR scheduler is less a booker of rooms than the custodian of a case's dependency graph. That graph is spread across a practice EHR, a hospital perioperative system, payer portals, a fax queue and one person's memory. Booking is the moment the graph gets an anchor date. Nearly everything that goes wrong afterward is either a dependency nobody chased or a change that never reached SPD, the vendor or the patient. This shifts how an assistant should be judged: by whether cases arrive ready and whether everyone affected hears about each change, not by how few turns it takes to book. It also explains why conversation belongs at the edges. Language is how the messy inputs arrive and how the chasing gets done, while feasibility, overview and judgment belong to code, grids and people.

The take-home's rubric centers on the booking conversation, but the evidence locates most of the value downstream. The two reconcile when the booking is built as the spine that creates the readiness graph and every seam the lifecycle will need, so the panel sees both a working path and a credible plan for the rest. The design also fits a forward-deployed role: site rules live in configuration, and autonomy is promoted per action as measured accuracy allows. The main uncertainties should be stated openly. No time-and-motion study shows how schedulers actually split their hours, first-person practitioner evidence is thin and dated, and nearly every outcome figure for agents in this space is self-reported. A pilot that timestamps request-to-booked, counts touches per case and tracks readiness at T-72 hours would produce the evidence this market lacks.
